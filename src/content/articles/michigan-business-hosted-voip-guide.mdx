For decades, Michigan businesses relied on traditional analog copper phone lines and expensive, closet-sized on-premise PBX boxes. Today, copper networks are rapidly being decommissioned by legacy carriers, maintenance costs are skyrocketing, and modern workforces need the freedom to work from anywhere.

**Hosted VoIP (Voice over Internet Protocol)** replaces outdated phone hardware with an enterprise cloud voice platform that operates over your existing internet connection.

In this guide, we break down exactly how Hosted VoIP works, what features matter most for Michigan organizations, and how to execute a seamless transition without losing a single customer call.

## Why Legacy Landlines Are Failing Michigan Businesses

Legacy phone systems present three critical liabilities for growing companies:

1. **Escalating Copper Line Rates**: Legacy phone carriers continue to raise analog POTS (Plain Old Telephone Service) line prices to nudge customers off aging infrastructure.
2. **Single Point of Failure**: When severe Michigan winter weather knocks out local power lines or phone cables, your on-premise PBX goes dark.
3. **Inflexibility for Hybrid Teams**: Traditional landlines tie employees to physical desks. Forwarding calls to personal cell phones is clunky and exposes personal numbers.

## How to Prepare for a Smooth VoIP Migration

Migrating to Hosted VoIP requires three straightforward steps:

### Step 1: Internet Bandwidth Assessment
Each active VoIP call requires approximately **100 Kbps** of upload and download bandwidth using high-definition audio codecs (G.711 / G.722). A quality dedicated fiber or high-speed broadband connection ensures crystal-clear voice clarity.

### Step 2: Quality of Service (QoS) Router Configuration
Your network router should prioritize voice packets over non-essential internet traffic (like large file downloads or streaming video), preventing jitter and dropped calls during peak internet usage.

### Step 3: Equipment Provisioning
Net Express pre-provisions your Polycom, Yealink, or Cisco IP desk phones before shipping. Simply plug them into your Ethernet network switch and start taking calls immediately.
