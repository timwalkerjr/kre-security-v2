When your business operations depend on cloud applications (Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, CRM systems, cloud ERPs) and VoIP phone calls, your internet connection is the backbone of daily revenue.

Many business owners wonder: *Is commercial broadband cable sufficient, or is it time to upgrade to dedicated fiber internet?*

Here is an objective breakdown of how the two technologies compare on bandwidth, reliability, and business impact.

## Symmetrical vs. Asymmetrical Speeds

The biggest difference between commercial cable and dedicated fiber lies in **upload bandwidth**:

- **Broadband Cable (Asymmetrical)**: Cable providers typically advertise high download speeds (e.g., 300 Mbps or 500 Mbps), but upload speeds are severely throttled — often to just 10 Mbps to 35 Mbps.
- **Dedicated Fiber (Symmetrical)**: Dedicated fiber delivers equal speed in both directions (e.g., 500/500 Mbps or 5 Gbps / 5 Gbps).

When multiple employees upload large CAD files, conduct video conference calls, back up databases to offsite servers, or route dozens of simultaneous VoIP voice streams, asymmetrical cable networks choke. Symmetrical fiber eliminates upload bottlenecks entirely.

## Shared Pipe vs. Dedicated Bandwidth

Cable internet operates on a shared neighborhood node. During peak business hours (between 1:00 PM and 4:00 PM), your available speed can fluctuate significantly as neighboring businesses consume network capacity.

**Dedicated Internet Access (DIA)** fiber provides a private, uncontended direct connection between your facility and the carrier backbone. Your contracted speed is guaranteed 24 hours a day, 365 days a year.

## Service Level Agreements (SLA)

| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Speed Guarantee** | "Up to" advertised speeds | 100% committed information rate |

| **Latency & Jitter** | Variable (25ms - 70ms) | Ultra-low (&lt;5ms local latency) |
| **Outage Repair Time** | Best-effort (24-48 hours) | 4-Hour MTTR Carrier SLA |
